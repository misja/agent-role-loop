# Principles

[loop.md](loop.md) is the sole routing norm. These principles explain its design; they do not establish another procedure. Core remains independent of model, provider and tracker.

## 1. Context isolation

Focused contexts can reduce interference from stale assumptions and exploration history. Independent reviewers receive the current artifact, applicable decisions and objective evidence, without the author's transcript. Initial reviewers do not see each other's verdicts. Repair reviewers start fresh with the explicit repair appendix; authors may retain context for the same targeted repair.

## 2. Explicit handoffs

Contracts C0–C7 make scope, evidence, decisions and next actions reviewable. Provide readable sources and exact artifact and norm versions. Missing facts are unavailable or assumptions, never plausible filler. Relevant human decisions and approved deviations belong in C5 core so isolated reviewers can apply them. Exploration transcripts and discarded attempts stay outside initial review.

## 3. Proportionality

Assign responsibilities and acceptance criteria before choosing extra agents. The orchestrator can triage, planners can investigate, and one qualified reviewer can cover a bounded change. Additional perspectives need a concrete reason; pragmatic judgment belongs to every role. LIGHT keeps independent review while omitting unnecessary plan artifacts. Oversized work is split. The loop bounds automatic repair and preserves counters across sessions; fewer roles or exhausted budgets cannot excuse uncovered criteria or unresolved blockers. These choices do not by themselves demonstrate cost savings or equal product quality.

## 4. Human judgment

Humans decide goals, scope, acceptable risk, required gates and merge. Record approval against concrete artifacts and carry it forward; do not repeatedly seek the same decision. Escalate new choices and exhausted repair allowances with a bounded next action. SHIP denotes readiness for the human merge decision.
